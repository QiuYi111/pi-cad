# Lighting and camera

Compute the subject's world-space bounding box after final transforms. Aim lights and camera at its visual center, and derive distance and light size from its extent.

Start with large area lights: key for form, weak fill for readable shadows, rim or strip lights for edge separation. Metal is revealed by reflected shapes, so position softboxes to create deliberate gradients. Add HDRI only when it improves reflections; do not let it flatten the key.

Use a three-quarter product view by default. A 70–120 mm lens usually preserves product proportions; use wider lenses only when context or tight space matters. Leave breathing room and keep the support surface visible when scale matters.

Depth of field must not hide engineering evidence. Focus on the main functional plane and keep critical interfaces sharp. Exploded and technical views normally need deeper focus.

Preview checks: subject occupies the intended frame area; no joint is hidden by overlap; bright edges retain texture; dark parts remain separated from the background; ground contact and shadows agree with the product's weight.
